# Chapter 1: Introduction

## 1.1 Background Literature

### 1.1.1 The Evolution of Supply Chain Transparency and Digital Product Passports (DPP)
In the modern landscape of global trade, particularly within resource-intensive sectors such as the rubber industry, the mandate for end-to-end traceability has transitioned from a supplementary corporate social responsibility initiative to an essential regulatory compliance requirement. The European Union’s push toward circular economies has spearheaded the conceptualization of the Digital Product Passport (DPP), a secure digital record that transparently encapsulates the lifecycle, origin, and material composition of physical goods. The fundamental objective of a DPP is to democratize product information, enabling downstream stakeholders and regulatory bodies to verify sustainability claims and quality metrics. However, the implementation of such comprehensive traceability mechanisms introduces a pivotal paradox: the inherent conflict between supply chain transparency and the protection of proprietary commercial intelligence.

Traditional traceability systems, predominantly reliant on monolithic database architectures or decentralized ledger technologies, generally operate on a binary visibility paradigm. Information is either globally broadcasted to the supply network or completely obscured. Consequently, when trading entities attempt to generate a DPP, they are often compelled to share underlying transactional documents—such as supplier invoices, quality inspection reports (QIR), and shipping manifests. This requirement inadvertently exposes sensitive trade secrets, including supplier identities, negotiated pricing stratagems, and intricate financial metadata, which are critical to maintaining competitive market advantages.

### 1.1.2 Cryptographic Paradigms in Secure Document Exchange
To mitigate unauthorized data exposure, existing secure trading platforms have extensively deployed standard cryptographic protocols, notably asymmetric (Public Key Infrastructure) and symmetric (e.g., AES) encryption algorithms. However, these implementations historically function at the perimeter as unconditional full-file encryption. While file-level envelope encryption effectively secures the document payload during transmission or at rest, implementing it blindly on all documents severely restricts backend operability and accessibility. If every document is automatically encrypted as an opaque blob, the central processing infrastructure cannot present non-sensitive public documents (like structural QIRs) seamlessly without forcing the user through computationally expensive decryption flows.

To allow for intermediate processing without compromising sensitive data, academic research has explored Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP) and selectively visible data structures. ZK paradigms dictate that the central server (or backend) verifying a transaction should not possess the ability to read the underlying sensitive data it is routing. Adapting these principles to enterprise architectures requires complex Relationship-Based Access Control (ReBAC) topologies and client-side encryption vaults, where the server acts merely as a blind conduit for ciphertext.

### 1.1.3 The Intersection of Machine Learning and Document Analytics
Concurrently, the advancement of Machine Learning (ML), particularly Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Optical Character Recognition (OCR), has revolutionized automated document ingestion. Large Language Models (LLMs) and Generative AI are now capable of complex key-value extraction from unstructured and semi-structured documents. Additionally, lightweight neural networks, such as those serialized via the Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) format, allow for high-efficiency, deterministic classification of text. Despite these advancements, the integration of AI directly into cryptographic pipelines—specifically dynamically leveraging ML to autonomously dictate full-file encryption policies based on the semantic context of the document—remains underexplored in operational supply chain mechanics.

## 1.2 Research Gap

The contemporary literature governing secure supply chain systems heavily emphasizes static, unconditional macro-level document encryption and distributed ledger transparency. However, a significant research gap persists in the domain of **dynamic, AI-driven conditional file privacy within automated trading systems**. 

Currently, commercial platforms force stakeholders into a restrictive dichotomy: either manually categorize documents before generating a Digital Product Passport (a non-scalable, error-prone administrative burden) or utilize blanket full-file encryption that neuters system searchability and dynamic data presentation for harmless documents. There is an absence of intelligent frameworks capable of autonomously analyzing a singular document, universally categorizing its content sensitivity, and dynamically applying surgical, full-file zero-knowledge encryption protocols only when legitimately required.

Furthermore, while decentralized identities and ZK architectures are theoretically robust, there is a lack of applied systems that seamlessly consolidate Generative AI OCR extraction, deterministic ML classification, and Zero-Knowledge client-side decryption vaults into a cohesive, mobile-accessible architecture. The necessity to generate a purely transparent DPP while mathematically proving the physical-to-digital continuity of the lot—without allowing the administrative backend any capacity to surveil the underlying conditionally encrypted transaction pricing—identifies a crucial void in current supply chain informatics literature.

## 1.3 Research Problem

The core problem this research addresses is the inability of centralized trading marketplaces to autonomously generate verifiable, compliant Digital Product Passports (DPP) from standard trading documents without inadvertently compromising the proprietary, confidential commercial data of the participating stakeholders, or conversely, resorting to inefficient "encrypt-everything" paradigms.

When buyers and exporters negotiate and formalize a lot transaction, they generate documents of varying sensitivity (e.g., highly sensitive invoices versus public quality reports). To legitimize the lot in a downstream market, the DPP must seamlessly present these documents. The challenge lies in creating an intelligent, frictionless system that can automatically ingest these documents, distinguish between universally publishable files and highly confidential files, and encrypt the latter in its entirety in a manner where even the database administrators cannot read it, yet still allow for verifiable physical auditing of the actual goods.

## 1.4 Research Objectives

To address the identified research problem and contribute novel methodologies to the field of secure supply chain informatics, this thesis proposes the design, implementation, and evaluation of the "Rubber Intelligence System": an AI-driven conditional Zero-Knowledge trading platform. The specific research objectives are meticulously defined as follows:

1. **To Architect a Dual-Layer AI Classification Engine:** To design and implement an intelligent extraction and classification pipeline seamlessly integrating Generative AI (for unstructured document OCR) with an ONNX-based neural network model to autonomously categorize entire documents into categorical confidentiality tiers (Public vs. Confidential) with high accuracy.
2. **To Develop an AI-Driven Zero-Knowledge Cryptographic Vault:** To engineer a conditional full-file encryption framework where sensitive documents, once classified by the AI, are secured utilizing AES-256-CBC, paired with cryptographically secure pseudo-random initialization vectors (CSPRNG IVs) and permanently nullified server-side keys. 
3. **To Establish a Secure, ReBAC-Governed Marketplace Module:** To construct a robust digital marketplace that governs the trading lifecycle through Relationship-Based Access Control. This objective includes securely distributing one-time client-side decryption keys, ensuring that locked transactional documents are accessible only to the precise physiological and cryptographic identity of the verified exporter.
4. **To Implement an Immutable Physical-to-Digital Interlock:** To formulate and validate a client-side physical verification mechanism utilizing dynamic, millisecond-truncated SHA-256 fingerprinting. This system must effectively bind the digital DPP state to the physical lot via a QR-code scanner interface, actively preventing sophisticated physical tampering, spoofing, and fraud in real-time.
